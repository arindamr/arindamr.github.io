Total economic cost by horizon

Economic cost, not cash out: every payment compounded forward at 5%, minus whatever the car or your equity in it is worth at the end. That is why paying cash never looks free.

Cost per year — the fairer comparison

Equivalent annual cost: each horizon's total discounted back and annuitised, so periods of different length can be compared honestly. Every car is cheapest per year at 4–5 years, and dearest at 2 — you never escape the front-loaded depreciation in two years.

Five ways to lose money that have nothing to do with which car you pick

Each of these is worth more than the difference between two trims. They are ordered by how much they cost and how easily they are missed.

1 · The 60-month PCP · costs £10,300–11,600

Every car here is quoted at 8.10% APR over 60 months, against 0% (or 2.90% on the Model Y Long Range) over 48. Tesla's promotion covers 24–48 month terms only, so asking for a five-year deal quietly moves you off it. On the Model 3 Long Range that is £10,681 of interest against zero.

If you want the car for five years, do not take a five-year PCP. Take the 48-month deal and settle the Optional Final Payment at month 48. Same car, same five years, and on the base Model 3 it is £12,514 cheaper.

2 · Paying cash · costs £5,500–11,000

Cash forfeits the £2,500 deposit contribution, which is only available if you finance, and it forfeits the return on capital you would otherwise keep invested. On the base Model 3 at 0%, the total payable equals the cash price exactly — so the borrowing is genuinely free and paying cash comes out £5,475 worse over two years, rising to £10,911 over ten.

The only honest argument for cash is not wanting a credit agreement on your file.

3 · The Personal Contract Hire headline · understates by 40–50%

Tesla quotes PCH as a monthly rental, but charges an advance rental on top of every one of the 24 payments — it does not buy out the first months. The Model Y Long Range shows £449/mo; the actual total payable is £16,374, an effective £682/mo. Broker leases usually work the other way round, which makes this easy to misread.

PCH is only competitive at exactly two years, and only on the Model 3 Long Range does it actually win. Because the advance recurs at every renewal, rolling PCH is the most expensive route at five years on every car.

4 · Options past £50,000 · costs £2,200

The Expensive Car Supplement is £440 a year in years two to six for zero-emission cars over £50,000 list — and list means price including options. The Model Y Premium Long Range starts at £48,990, leaving only £1,010 of headroom. A £1,300 paint upgrade on that car really costs about £3,500.

The Model 3 Long Range has £5,010 of room and the two base cars are nowhere near it.

5 · Over-sizing the deposit at 0% · costs £700–900

At 0% APR the total payable is fixed regardless of deposit, so a bigger deposit only moves your own money forward in time. On the base Model 3, dropping the cash deposit from £6,150 to nil raises the monthly from £249 to £380 but saves £719 over five years, because the borrowing is free and your capital keeps earning.

This reverses if the promotion lapses: at a real APR the interest you avoid roughly equals the return you forgo, and the deposit stops mattering. It is specific to 0%.

And one thing that is not a trap: the mileage cap

At 0% every mileage option totals the same amount payable — a bigger cap simply moves money out of the Optional Final Payment and into the monthly. Taking 8,000 miles and paying the 16.8p/mile excess saves about £70 if you settle and keep the car, but costs £599 if you hand it back. Take the 10,000-mile cap: the £70 is not worth an 8:1 asymmetry, and the higher cap also raises your hand-back threshold, so it buys a more valuable option, not a less valuable one.

Where this lands

You asked for balance rather than a single winner, so this is framed as who each car suits. But two things are not close, and it would be evasive to pretend otherwise.

The financing decision is settled, whichever car you choose. Take the 48-month PCP with the £2,500 contribution, put down the smallest deposit Tesla will accept, choose the 10,000-mile cap, and plan to settle the Optional Final Payment at month 48. That combination is cheapest on all four cars at every horizon from three years out. The car choice is a separate, and much more personal, question.
The Model Y Premium Long Range is hard to defend on numbers alone. It is the only one of the four Tesla will not give 0% on, so you pay £3,468 of interest that the others avoid. It costs £6,379 more than the Model 3 Long Range over four years while offering 88 fewer miles of range, and it starts £1,010 from a £2,200 tax cliff. If you need the SUV body it is the only Long Range SUV here — but that body is the entire case for it.

The honest summary, car by car

Three things this page deliberately does not cover

Salary sacrifice. At a 4% benefit-in-kind rate it beats every route on this page by a wide margin — typically enough to make a Long Range car cost less than a base car bought personally. If Exeter or your employer offers a scheme, this entire comparison is the wrong question and you should price that first.
All-wheel drive and Performance trims. Model 3 Long Range AWD (£399/mo) and Model Y Long Range AWD (£449/mo), plus both Performance cars, were not swept. They sit above the trims here and, on the Model Y side, further past the £50,000 supplement threshold. If AWD matters for where you live, they are worth quoting — the same finance logic applies.
Used and ex-demonstrator cars. A one- to two-year-old Tesla has already taken the steepest part of the depreciation curve, which is the single largest cost on this page. That advantage is real and can exceed everything discussed here. The trade-off is that you forfeit the 0% finance and the £2,500 contribution, and used PCP rates are materially higher. Worth pricing properly rather than assuming either way.
